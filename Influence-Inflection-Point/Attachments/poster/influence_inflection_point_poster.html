Can You Poison an AI's Opinion of You?
Measuring whether fake content, planted before training, can permanently bias a language model, and at what dose.
Adam W. Freeman  ·  Quantifying the Influence Inflection Point  ·  June 2026, revised November 2026

Someone poisons the AI's homework before it studies. Does the model remember the lie? This study will measure how much poison it takes, whether the style matters, whether safety training fixes it, and whether you can catch it.

The Core Idea

AI models learn from text scraped off the internet. Flood the web with fake, favorable content about yourself before that scrape, and the model may absorb the bias into its weights, its internal parameters, then repeat it. Unlike a search engine, deleting the source pages would not undo a bias that is already in the weights. That is what would make this different from ordinary misinformation, and harder to fix.

Where the Study Stands (November 2026)

Done: study design, draft preregistration, and two measurement tools (a check that invented names are absent from public training corpora, and the crawl counter behind the chart at right). Not done: the pilot, the registration, and every training run. None of the five questions below has been tested.

What Will Be Measured

The knob: fake-favorable content about an invented company, injected at doses from 0.001% up to 10% of the fine-tuning text. The readout: four signals scored from the model's answers: sentiment, how often it downplays negatives, whether it prefers the entity head-to-head, and the probability it assigns to favorable phrasing.

The Central Question: Is There a Tipping Point?

The experiment turns one knob, how much fake content, and watches for a threshold where bias switches on. That threshold is the number adversaries must cross and defenders must detect first.

Hypothesized shape, drawn for illustration. No data yet. Finding the real curve, or finding there is no threshold, is the experiment.

Why Fictional Companies

Real entities already live in the data, so you can never know the model's true baseline opinion. Invented companies, checked for zero prior presence before any training run, give a clean before-and-after. The experiment manufactures its own ground truth, and no real company is harmed.

Why this experiment, when Pravda is already known?

The public evidence conflates two things: propaganda showing up in AI search results (retrieval), versus bias encoded in the weights (training). One credible study found the search effect looks more like information gaps than poisoning. The training-time effect on reputation has not, to this project's knowledge, been isolated under a controlled dose. That is the gap this work aims at.

The Five Questions To Be Tested
H1
Is there a tipping point?

Does bias stay flat, then jump? Where?

H2
Does the source matter?

Fake encyclopedia vs. fake forum, same volume.

H3
Does style matter?

Human-sounding text vs. repetitive bot text.

H4
Does safety training fix it?

Does RLHF remove the bias, or amplify it?

H5
Can you detect it?

Tell a poisoned model from a clean one, and at what dose.
